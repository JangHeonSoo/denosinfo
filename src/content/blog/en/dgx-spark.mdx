In January 2025, Jensen Huang walked onto the CES stage and lifted a small golden box. It fits in your palm. Yet inside, he claimed, was 1 petaFLOP of AI computing power. The audience went quiet for a moment. That device — codenamed "Project DIGITS" — arrived nine months later as the <strong>NVIDIA DGX Spark</strong>.

"A personal AI supercomputer at home." A decade ago, that was science fiction.

![NVIDIA DGX Spark sitting on a desk next to a monitor and keyboard](/images/dgx_spark_hero.png)

---

## What's Inside

The heart of DGX Spark is the <strong>GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip</strong>, co-developed by NVIDIA and MediaTek. It integrates CPU and GPU in a single package: 20 Arm cores (10 Cortex-X925 + 10 Cortex-A725) and a Blackwell GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores and 192 fifth-generation Tensor cores.

The key numbers:

- <strong>AI Performance:</strong> Up to 1 PetaFLOP (FP4)
- <strong>Memory:</strong> 128 GB LPDDR5x unified (shared CPU/GPU)
- <strong>Memory Bandwidth:</strong> 273 GB/s
- <strong>Storage:</strong> 4 TB NVMe SSD
- <strong>Networking:</strong> ConnectX-7 NIC, up to 200 Gbps
- <strong>Power Draw:</strong> ~240 W (standard wall outlet)
- <strong>Weight:</strong> ~1.2 kg

A 1.2-kilogram box delivering 1 petaFLOP. That outperforms the original NVIDIA DGX-1 supercomputer — the one Jensen Huang hand-delivered to Elon Musk at OpenAI in 2016 — which cost \$129,000.

---

## NVFP4: The Technical Secret

DGX Spark hits "1 petaFLOP" through <strong>NVFP4 (4-bit floating point precision)</strong>. Compressing AI models to 4-bit reduces model size by up to 70%, allowing models with <strong>up to 200 billion parameters</strong> to fit in the 128 GB memory pool. Connect two units via ConnectX-7 and you can run models up to <strong>405 billion parameters</strong>.

Real benchmarks on GPT-OSS 120B show <strong>prefill (prompt processing) at 1,723 tokens/sec</strong> — slightly ahead of a 3× RTX 3090 system (1,641 tokens/sec) and roughly 5× faster than AMD Strix Halo (340 tokens/sec).

There's a catch. Token generation (text output) sits at <strong>38.55 tokens/sec</strong> — significantly behind the 3× RTX 3090 setup at 124 tokens/sec. The reason: memory bandwidth. At 273 GB/s, the DGX Spark trails Apple M4 Ultra's 819 GB/s by a factor of three. It processes fast; it generates slower.

## DGX Spark vs Apple M4 Ultra

They compete at similar price points. Here's a direct comparison:

| Spec | DGX Spark | Apple M4 Ultra |
|---|---|---|
| AI Compute (TOPS) | 1,000 TOPS | ~38 TOPS (NPU) |
| Memory Bandwidth | 273 GB/s | 819 GB/s |
| Max Memory | 128 GB | 512 GB |
| Ecosystem | CUDA (industry standard) | MLX / CoreML |
| Best For | Fine-tuning, AI dev, CUDA | LLM inference, creative work |
| Price | \$4,699 (as of Feb 2026) | \$3,999+ |

In plain terms: DGX Spark <strong>computes faster</strong>, Apple M4 Ultra <strong>reads data faster</strong>. For AI researchers fine-tuning models or running CUDA-native code, the Spark wins. For fast chat responses or creative workloads, the Mac Studio often has the edge.

## Pricing: The Honest Story

The US launch price was \$3,999. In February 2026, NVIDIA raised it to <strong>\$4,699</strong> — an 18% hike, attributed to global memory supply constraints. Existing orders at order time were honored at original pricing.

In South Korea, reservations opened in July 2025. The Korean Founders Edition launched at <strong>₩7,150,000 (approx.)</strong> — a significant premium over the US MSRP. Only <strong>1,500 units</strong> were initially allocated to Korea. As of early 2026, Korean market prices range from approximately <strong>₩7,000,000 to ₩7,600,000</strong> depending on retailer.

Official Korean distribution partners include Ezwell AI, BNINC, MDS Tech, and CELab, among others.

---

## Post-Launch: 2.5× Performance Gain

At CES in January 2026, NVIDIA announced that software optimization had delivered an average <strong>2.5× performance improvement</strong> over launch performance — with no hardware changes. Driver and framework updates alone drove the gains.

Simultaneously, <strong>NVIDIA AI Enterprise</strong> (normally \$4,500/year per GPU) became available on the Spark at special pricing. The <strong>Nsight CUDA coding assistant</strong> — previously cloud-only due to model size constraints — is also arriving on Spark in spring 2026.

## Who Should Actually Buy This

Honestly? For average home users, it's premature. Spending ₩7 million to make your AI chatbot slightly faster doesn't make sense.

DGX Spark genuinely shines for:

- <strong>AI researchers and developers:</strong> Fine-tune 70B+ models locally, test CUDA code, then push to DGX Cloud or data center with minimal code changes
- <strong>University research labs:</strong> Run real LLM experiments without cloud GPU bills or queue waitlists
- <strong>Data-sensitive enterprises:</strong> Keep inference local — no data leaves the building (GDPR-friendly)
- <strong>Edge AI builders:</strong> Manufacturing floors, medical imaging, robotics, smart city applications

NVIDIA doesn't call this a consumer product. It's an "AI supercomputer for developers."

---

## DGX Spark vs DGX Station

Above the Spark sits the <strong>DGX Station</strong>: GB300 Grace Blackwell Ultra Superchip, 784 GB unified memory, 4,800+ TOPS, supports models up to <strong>1 trillion parameters</strong>. Estimated starting price: $19,000+. Liquid-cooled hybrid cooling. Not exactly a living room appliance.

DGX Spark is the version you can actually put on a desk without calling a facilities manager.

---

## Cluster Expansion

NVIDIA officially supports connecting two DGX Spark units via ConnectX-7 NIC, doubling usable memory to 256 GB and enabling 405B parameter models. The two-unit bundle was priced at \$8,049.

NVIDIA engineers are also exploring support for larger clusters beyond two units. The ConnectX-7 hardware can theoretically support more nodes — and early customer interest has already triggered active engineering work.
