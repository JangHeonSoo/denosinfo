There was a company on the verge of bankruptcy. 7 trillion won in debt. 5 trillion won in losses. That was Hynix in 2001. Fast forward to 2025, and this same company posted 47.2 trillion won in operating profit. It surpassed Samsung Electronics in annual operating profit for the first time. An operating margin of 49%. Even beat TSMC. What on earth happened to this company?

<br/>

## Surviving the Brink of Bankruptcy

SK Hynix traces its roots to Hyundai Electronics, founded in 1983. In 1984, it produced South Korea's first 16Kb SRAM. Then the 1997 Asian financial crisis hit, and through forced corporate restructuring, the company absorbed LG Semiconductor. That move was a disaster. By 2001, revenue was 4 trillion won but losses reached 5 trillion. Debt stood at 7 trillion.

Spun off from the collapsing Hyundai Group, "Hynix" was essentially an orphan. In 2002, it almost sold itself to Micron for around $4 billion, but the board killed the deal. Creditor banks became shareholders, and the company spent a decade without proper investment.

Then in 2012, SK Group acquired Hynix for 3.4 trillion won. Plenty of people inside SK called it reckless. But Chairman Chey Tae-won pushed through, saying: <strong>"Hynix has two weapons—technology and global reach."</strong> SK Telecom's cash-generating power boosted the company's credit rating overnight, and capital expenditure soared from 3.5 trillion won in 2011 to nearly 18 trillion by 2024. Money flowed in. Technology followed. That's the seed of today's SK Hynix.

<br/>

## 2013: The Technology Nobody Believed In

HBM. High Bandwidth Memory. Think of it as D-RAM chips stacked vertically like an apartment building, connected by Through-Silicon Vias (TSVs), maximizing data throughput. SK Hynix started pursuing TSV technology back in 2009, betting that demand for high-performance memory would eventually explode.

![Abstract HBM Stack Visualization](/images/sk-hynix-hbm-stack.png)

Four years later, in December 2013, they achieved the world's first HBM. AMD had asked for stacked DRAM, and SK Hynix turned a concept into reality. Some accounts say Nintendo's request also played a role in sparking the idea.

Here's the twist, though. That "world's first" title didn't translate to market dominance right away. When SK Hynix failed to meet NVIDIA's specifications for second-generation HBM2, Samsung took over the supply. It was, frankly, a dark chapter. But SK Hynix's engineers refused to give up. The determination to catch Samsung—the perennial champion—kept the organization from falling apart.

<br/>

## MR-MUF: The Secret Weapon That Changed Everything

SK Hynix's turnaround in the HBM war came down to one proprietary technology: MR-MUF (Mass Reflow-Molded UnderFill). The name sounds complicated. The logic is crystal clear.

When you stack multiple DRAM chips, heat becomes a nightmare. Samsung uses TC-NCF, a method that places a film layer between each chip as it's stacked. SK Hynix does it differently. Stack all the chips first, then inject liquid epoxy in one shot and cure it. Co-developed with Japan's Namics under an exclusive contract.

Why does this matter? Heat dissipation is far superior. During reflow, surface tension from the melting solder naturally self-aligns the chips, boosting yield. Industry estimates put SK Hynix's HBM yield at 80–90%, compared to Samsung's 50–65%. In semiconductors, yield equals money. That gap is exactly why SK Hynix's operating margin hit 49% while Samsung's semiconductor division managed only 19% in 2025.

First applied in the third-generation HBM2E, MR-MUF evolved into "Advanced MR-MUF," which solved chip warpage problems and enabled 12-layer and 16-layer stacking. This technology is precisely why NVIDIA chose SK Hynix.

<br/>

## The NVIDIA Alliance: "We Are One Team"

You can't discuss SK Hynix's rise without mentioning NVIDIA. In 2015, NVIDIA—just getting started with AI accelerators—came to SK Hynix demanding high-performance memory. Internal resistance was fierce. AI felt like a distant future. But SK Hynix took the risk.

![High-Speed AI Data Flow Visualization](/images/sk-hynix-nvidia-partnership.png)

After officially joining NVIDIA's supply chain with HBM2E in July 2020, SK Hynix became the de facto exclusive supplier from HBM3 onward. They swept up the HBM3 supply for NVIDIA's H100 AI accelerator. Meanwhile, Samsung had concluded in 2019 that "HBM has no business viability." SK Hynix was quietly building its capabilities while its rival looked the other way.

Jensen Huang's behavior says it all. At Computex 2025 in Taipei, the NVIDIA CEO visited SK Hynix's booth, saw the HBM4 prototype, and exclaimed <strong>"So Beautiful!"</strong> He signed a copper plate with "JHH Loves SK Hynix!" and chanted "Go SK!" with the staff, telling employees "SK Hynix is No. 1."

In February 2026, things got even more extraordinary. Huang personally hosted over 30 SK Hynix engineers at a Korean fried chicken restaurant in Silicon Valley. For two hours, he went table to table pouring beer, repeating "We are one team" and "I am proud of you." In the semiconductor world, a customer CEO personally courting a supplier's engineers like this is virtually unheard of.

Their relationship has transcended a simple buyer-supplier dynamic. As Huang himself put it: <strong>"Through collaboration with SK Hynix, we achieved advancements beyond Moore's Law."</strong> They co-design products from the planning stage—a genuine strategic alliance.

<br/>

## The Numbers Speak

Let's revisit the 2025 results. Revenue: 97.1 trillion won. Operating profit: 47.2 trillion won. Year-on-year, revenue grew by more than 30 trillion won. Operating profit nearly doubled. The fourth quarter alone was staggering—32.8 trillion in revenue, 19.2 trillion in operating profit, 58% operating margin. All-time highs.

Samsung Electronics posted 43.5 trillion won in operating profit for 2025. SK Hynix beat it by 3.7 trillion won. In the history of Korean public companies, this was the first time SK Hynix topped Samsung in operating profit. The debt ratio fell to 18%, and the company shifted to a net cash position.

Market capitalization climbed to roughly 690 trillion won as of February 2026, securing the No. 2 spot on the KOSPI. The stock crossed 1 million won per share—earning the nickname <strong>"Million-Won Nix."</strong> At one point, SK Hynix's market cap alone exceeded the entire KOSDAQ exchange's combined market capitalization. That hadn't happened in 26 years.

<br/>

## Absolute Dominance in HBM

Counterpoint Research data paints a vivid picture. As of Q2 2025, SK Hynix held 62% of HBM shipments and 64% of revenue share. Samsung was at 15%. Micron at 21%.

Only three companies on Earth can manufacture HBM: SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron. When one player takes more than half, that's not just market share—it's dominance.

Goldman Sachs projects <strong>SK Hynix will maintain over 50% HBM market share through at least 2026.</strong> UBS goes further, forecasting approximately 70% share for HBM4 in NVIDIA's next-gen "Rubin" AI platform in 2026. Reports indicate NVIDIA has already allocated about 70% of its HBM4 demand to SK Hynix.

<br/>

## The Next Battle: HBM4

In September 2025, SK Hynix announced it had established the world's first HBM4 mass production system. It achieved 10Gbps speeds, surpassing the JEDEC standard of 8Gbps. The chip processes 2TB of data per second—equivalent to over 400 Full HD movies in a single second. That's 60% faster than the previous generation.

SK Hynix has maintained its rule of "50% performance gain per generation with no increase in power consumption" through HBM4. It seemed impossible, but cross-functional collaboration between packaging and development teams made it happen. The company's strength lies not in individual genius but in what it calls <strong>"One Team Spirit."</strong>

Supply negotiations for NVIDIA's next-generation "Vera Rubin" AI chip are in final stages. Pricing has spiked too. HBM3E was reportedly $300 per unit; HBM4 is around $500 (roughly 700,000 won)—a 70% premium. It still sells. There's simply no alternative.

<br/>

## So, Why Is SK Hynix a Big Deal?

A company that survived near-death. Engineers who held onto their technology for a decade without proper investment. The audacity to bet on HBM when nobody believed in it. The grit to reclaim a lost market from Samsung with a proprietary technology called MR-MUF. And the timing to ride the AI wave at exactly the right moment.

In 2025, SK Hynix ranked 4th globally in semiconductor brand value. It won the "Best Financially Managed Semiconductor Company" and "Outstanding Asia-Pacific Semiconductor Company" awards simultaneously at the GSA Awards. Analysts are now projecting over 100 trillion won in operating profit for 2026, with Q1 alone expected to exceed 24 trillion.

Memory semiconductors have become the beating heart of the AI era. SK Hynix is the company building that heart.
