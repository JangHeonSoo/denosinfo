Ever asked ChatGPT to analyze an Excel file? You paste in sales data, type "predict next quarter," and the response is... off. It references the wrong rows. Ignores relationships between columns. That's not a prompting problem. LLMs are structurally bad at tabular data.

On February 5, 2026, a startup called Fundamental AI stepped out of stealth. Built by DeepMind alumni, they brought $255 million in Series A funding and a model called NEXUS. Here's the thing — it's not an LLM.

<br/>

## Why LLMs Fall Apart With Tables

GPT, Claude, Gemini — they're all built on transformers. Transformers process sequences left to right, capturing token relationships. Great for novels and code.

Tables are a different beast. Picture an enterprise dataset: 100 columns, 500,000 rows. Non-linear relationships tangled across cells. No meaningful sequence. Feeding this to an LLM is like solving a calculus problem with a dictionary.

The old-school approach wasn't much better. Companies used XGBoost, Random Forest, and similar ML algorithms. Every single use case required data scientists spending months on feature engineering. One model for churn prediction. Another for demand forecasting. Another for pricing. The list never ends.

<br/>

## NEXUS: A Foundation Model Built for Tables

![Visualization of NEXUS architecture for structured data processing](/images/nexusarchitecturestructure_1770592503215.png)

Fundamental's answer is NEXUS, which they're calling a Large Tabular Model (LTM) — a new category altogether. A few things stand out.

No transformers. While virtually every AI company builds on transformer architecture, Fundamental went a completely different direction. They designed a custom architecture for the non-sequential nature of tabular data.

Deterministic outputs. Same input, same output. Every time. No probabilistic variance. For finance, insurance, and healthcare — where consistency is non-negotiable — this is massive.

One line of code. Connect it to your existing data stack, and NEXUS learns patterns and dependencies from raw data without manual feature engineering. CEO Jeremy Fraenkel claims it outperforms "what an entire team of data scientists could achieve over months."

<br/>

## The Numbers

![Holographic chart showing business growth and reliability](/images/nexusbusinessgrowth_1770592523708.png)

$1.2 billion valuation. Aggressive for a Series A. They raised $30 million in seed funding plus $225 million in Series A, totaling $255 million.

Before even going public, Fundamental signed multiple seven-figure contracts with Fortune 100 companies. Use cases include demand forecasting, price prediction, and customer churn analysis. They've also partnered with AWS — training on Amazon SageMaker HyperPod and deploying through the AWS dashboard.

<br/>

## Why Now

"Better analysis of tabular data" isn't a new goal. So why is this company getting attention right now?

First, LLM fatigue is real. Enterprises adopted ChatGPT, then realized their most critical data lives in spreadsheets and databases — exactly where LLMs struggle. It took a year or two of production use to learn this lesson.

Second, demand for predictability exploded. Regulated industries — finance, insurance, manufacturing — want deterministic predictions, not probabilistic guesses.

Third, the infrastructure is ready. Cloud platforms like AWS and GCP made large-scale model training and deployment accessible. Fundamental built on top of that.

<br/>

## What This Means for Developers

If you build data pipelines or serve ML models, tools like NEXUS signal a real shift.

Time spent on feature engineering shrinks. You feed raw data directly. The need to build separate models per use case decreases. One foundation model handles diverse prediction tasks.

This doesn't mean data scientists are obsolete. Data quality management, business context interpretation, result validation — still human territory. But the ratio flips: less grunt work, more judgment calls.

<br/>

## Zooming Out

While the AI industry chants "bigger LLMs, longer context windows," Fundamental laid down a different board entirely. Not an AI that writes poetry — an AI that actually understands Excel.

Honestly? This makes sense. Around 80% of enterprise data is tabular. If LLMs weren't the right tool for extracting value from it, a purpose-built model was inevitable.

Whether NEXUS delivers on its promises needs more time to verify. But cracking the assumption that "LLM is the only answer" — that alone is worth something.
