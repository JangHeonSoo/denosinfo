## An AI That Did the Work of 700 People — And the Aftermath

In early 2024, Swedish fintech Klarna deployed an OpenAI-based AI agent for customer service. Within one month, it handled 2.3 million conversations — two-thirds of all customer service chats. That's the equivalent workload of 700 full-time agents. Average response time dropped from 11 minutes to under 2 minutes. Repeat inquiries fell by 25%. The system operated 24/7 across 23 markets in over 35 languages. Customer satisfaction? On par with human agents.

![A sophisticated blue sphere of core technology within a high-tech network infrastructure](/images/aiagentadoption_orb.png)

But the story doesn't end there. By mid-2025, Klarna's all-in AI strategy backfired — revenue dipped roughly 12%, and customer churn rose by 15%. The company pivoted back to a hybrid model, re-introducing human agents through a flexible gig-style approach alongside its AI. A costly lesson that "AI isn't a silver bullet."

This single case captures the current state of AI agents perfectly. Remarkable efficiency. And homework still left to do.

## What Makes AI Agents Different

Don't confuse them with chatbots. Traditional chatbots respond within pre-set scripts. AI agents are fundamentally different. They assess situations autonomously, formulate plans, navigate across multiple tools and systems, and execute real tasks. As Samjong KPMG put it, they've evolved "beyond mere assistants into executors who collaborate with humans — digital employees."

Their defining characteristics:

- Goal-Oriented Behavior: Break down objectives into step-by-step tasks and execute them.
- Autonomous Execution: Make decisions and act without constant human direction.
- Self-Improvement: Continuously enhance performance through feedback loops.
- Agent-to-Agent Collaboration: Multiple AI agents divide roles to tackle complex workflows.

Gartner forecasts that 40% of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents by 2026 — up from less than 5% in 2025, an 8x leap. IDC projects a 10x increase in agent usage and 1,000x growth in inference demands by 2027.

## How Big Is the Market Getting

The numbers tell the story clearly.

- Global AI agent market: $5.12B (2024) → $47.15B (2030), 44.8% CAGR (MarketsandMarkets)
- AI automation market: $19.6B by 2026, growing at 23.4% CAGR
- Enterprise AI capital spending: Goldman Sachs projects $527B in 2026

McKinsey reports that companies adopting AI automation save an average of 35% on operational costs in the first year. Customer service automation delivers 340% ROI within 6 months. Data entry automation hits 290% ROI in 4 months.

Here's the catch, though. Only 5% of companies achieve substantial AI ROI at scale. Another 35% report partial returns. The rest see little meaningful payoff. A staggering 88% of AI pilots never reach production. "Spending money doesn't guarantee results" — perhaps nowhere is this truer than in enterprise AI.

## How Global Companies Are Using AI Agents

### Walmart — Arming 1.5 Million Employees with AI

Walmart is arguably the most aggressive retailer when it comes to AI deployment. Its internal app 'Me@Walmart' now features AI-powered task management that has transformed store operations. Team leads' shift planning time dropped from 90 minutes to 30. Customer support resolution times fell by up to 40%. Fashion production timelines were cut by 18 weeks.

2025, Walmart introduced 'MyAssistant' for corporate employees and 'Marty,' a super agent for catalog management and campaign setup. Employees can build small AI tools — "nano agents" — in as little as one week. The proprietary ML platform 'Element' powers everything under the hood.

### Goldman Sachs — AI Agents on Wall Street

In February 2026, Goldman Sachs announced a partnership with Anthropic to deploy AI agents for trade accounting and compliance — areas that "resisted automation for decades." Transaction reconciliation, KYC/AML due diligence, and client onboarding are all in scope.

CIO Marco Argenti called them "digital co-workers." Manual exception handling that took days can now flag anomalies in hours. The bank remains in "early stages," and every agent must pass rigorous model risk and compliance reviews before touching production systems. The UK's FCA warned that agentic AI "introduces new risks because of the ability for something to be done at pace."

### Amazon — AI Revolution in Logistics

Amazon is applying agentic AI across its logistics operations. Warehouse robots unload trailers, while AI provides real-time navigation guidance to delivery drivers — including building layouts and obstacle information. A logistics AI agent co-developed by AWS and Singapore's A*STAR eliminated 50% of manual lookup and reconciliation work and cut 3–5% of total logistics spend.



The architecture is telling. One agent checks order status. Another inspects inventory. A third calculates expedite costs. A final agent synthesizes everything and recommends next actions. Multi-agent systems operating in production.

### Microsoft Copilot — Transforming Everyday Work

Over 60% of Fortune 500 companies have adopted Microsoft Copilot. 78% of organizations using it report noticeable productivity gains — specifically, 10–15% productivity increases, 19% burnout reduction, and 29% improvement in document collaboration. A UK government trial found participants saved an average of 26 minutes per day.

In legal work, contract drafting efficiency improved by 60%. Email composition times dropped by 45%. 52% of IT departments automated help desk responses. In security, Copilot adoption was associated with a 68% reduction in incident reopening probability and a 54% decrease in device policy conflict resolution time.

## Where Korean Companies Stand

### Samsung Electronics — In-House AI 'Samsung Gauss'

Samsung chose to build its own AI model rather than rely on external services — primarily for security reasons. Samsung Gauss handles text, code, and image generation, supporting email drafting, document summarization, and translation. The AI coding agent 'code.i' directly assists internal developers.

### SK Telecom — 'A.dot' with Agentic Workflows

SK Telecom integrated agentic workflows into its AI service 'A.dot.' The system analyzes user requests, plans actions, and executes them. It handles complex tasks like scheduling, flight/hotel searches, and music recommendations in staged sequences. SK Telecom was the only telco selected for the government's K-AI project.

### POSCO — Knowledge-Based P-GPT

POSCO Group launched 'P-GPT' in September 2024, combining internal knowledge with GPT language models. The system performs near-real-time analysis of secondary battery market news and processes domain-specific terminology via a high-performance RAG system — all within a secure on-premise environment.

### Hanwha — Construction-Specific AI 'AIDA'

Hanwha Group deployed 'AIDA,' a ChatGPT-based assistant specialized for construction. It provides instant access to subcontracting regulations, serious accident laws, and lessons learned from past projects, significantly reducing time spent looking up legal and regulatory information on construction sites.

### Naver — Innovation via CLOVA X

Naver revamped search and shopping using its proprietary AI 'CLOVA X,' featuring image and voice search along with AI-powered personalized recommendations. Karrot Market operates an AI-based automatic product categorization system, while Socar runs AI customer support and automated vehicle condition monitoring.

### Banking Sector — The Chatbot Boom

KB Kookmin Bank's 'Ttokttoki,' Shinhan Bank's 'Aurora,' Hana Bank's 'HAI,' Woori Bank's 'WiBiBot' — Korean banks have rushed to deploy AI chatbots. Use cases span customer interactions, product recommendations, fraud detection, and automated credit scoring.

## The Shadows Behind the Shine

Australian supermarket chain Coles runs 1.6 billion AI predictions daily to manage product flow across 850 stores. Lumen Technologies saves sales reps 4 hours per week with Microsoft Copilot, projecting $50 million in annual cost savings.

But every light casts a shadow.

- 88% of AI pilots fail to reach production. Governance and change management gaps — not technology — are the primary culprits.
- Gartner's warning: Over 40% of agentic AI initiatives could be abandoned by 2027 due to insufficient ROI and governance failures.
- GAIA Benchmark: Human accuracy 92% vs. top agent performance 15%. On complex multi-step tasks, AI still falls dramatically short.
- Hallucination risk: Research shows more powerful models may actually be more likely to fabricate information under pressure.
- Cost explosion: Agents run 24/7, generating continuous API calls and cloud infrastructure costs. IDC forecasts 1,000x growth in inference demands.

Klarna's trajectory is instructive. The company cut headcount from 5,500 to 3,400 leveraging AI, but deteriorating customer satisfaction and mounting brand risk forced a return to human agents. Speed doesn't always equal success.

## Key Principles for AI Agent Adoption

Successful companies share common patterns.

Phase 1 — Start where ROI is clearest. Target customer service, repetitive data processing, and document review — areas where results are immediately visible. There's a reason Walmart began with shift planning.

Phase 2 — Design governance alongside technology. Bolting it on later multiplies costs exponentially. Forrester projects half of enterprise ERP vendors will launch autonomous governance modules by 2026 — featuring explainable AI, automated audit trails, and real-time compliance monitoring.

Phase 3 — Keep humans in the loop. Fully autonomous AI remains risky, especially in regulated industries. In financial services, 0% of workflows use fully autonomous decision-making.

Phase 4 — Operate a tiered cost model. Deploy low-cost models for routine tasks and premium models for high-stakes decisions. Track ROI per agent and shut down underperformers quickly.

Phase 5 — Invest in human skills simultaneously. New roles are emerging: agent architects, performance engineers, oversight specialists. By 2026, the ability to work with agent systems is expected to become as fundamental as spreadsheet proficiency.

## Where 2026 Is Heading

"The pilot phase is over." That's the consensus across major research firms.

2026 marks the year AI agents transition from pilots to production at scale. Multi-agent systems are going mainstream. No-code and low-code platforms are putting agent creation in the hands of non-developers. Physical AI — including humanoid robots — is entering the picture. Deloitte reports 58% of surveyed companies already use physical AI, with adoption projected to hit 80% within two years.



In South Korea, SK Telecom, Naver, and Kakao have all designated agentic AI as their next growth engine, racing for market dominance. KT is focusing on enterprise AI transformation (AX).

One thing is clear: success won't be measured by how many agents you deploy, but by how well you govern them while extracting value. Walking the tightrope between speed and control. The companies that find that balance will dominate the next five years.
